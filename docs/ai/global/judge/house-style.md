# House style — topics, people, and notation

## Topics we do not cover

- The private life, relationships, controversies, incidents, or lawsuits of real celebrities and public figures.
- Ongoing political issues and inter-Korean military issues.
- Promotion of a specific agency or brand.
- A public figure may be mentioned only for verifiable facts about their public work, such as a debut year or an official award. An episode centered on one person is out of scope.
- A private person from an excerpt is a case, not a public figure. The script tells their account of an everyday event or practice by the role the story needs (a student, an office worker, a shop owner), with no name and no health, family, or other details the point does not need. Anonymity is the rule for such a case, not a reason to remove it.

## Sources in lines

- Lines never name or point to a source, by name, kind, or year. The episode shows its source list separately. Edits keep this: keep the condition, not the source.

## People, music, and drama

- No private matters, controversies, or unverified claims about people known to the public by name.
- No song lyrics and no lines from dramas or films, read or quoted. K-pop and drama episodes explain in the speakers' own words.

## Romanization

Apply these in order.

1. Names of people, groups, companies, organizations, and works: the official English name in established use. For a person with no confirmed official spelling: family name, space, given name written as one word (a hyphen inside the given name is allowed).
2. Places, cultural heritage, tourist sites, food, and other public terms: the government guidelines for foreign-language notation of public terms — the romanized name followed by its meaning, such as Gyeongbokgung Palace.
3. Other Korean words: the Revised Romanization of Korean, with the meaning given at first use. A word first heard in the fixed intro template (where the template leaves no room for a meaning) has its meaning given in the first explainer turn of the Opening; that counts as first use.

- Dokdo and East Sea are written in the Korean form.
- One spelling for one thing, within the episode and across episodes. The pronunciation list holds the fixed spellings. A name with an established English form (Seoul, Busan, Jeju, a company or group in its official English spelling) needs no entry in the list.
- Numbers, years, and currency use ordinary English forms. Korean won amounts are written in KRW only, with no dollar conversion.
- A Korean word gets its meaning right after its first use, and again when it returns after two sections without being heard. Between those two points the script may say the Korean word alone or its English name. At most two new Korean words per turn.
- The dictionary meaning of a common English word (a food, a tool, an illness, a material) needs no excerpt. It is a finding only when it is wrong or when it adds a fact about Korea.
