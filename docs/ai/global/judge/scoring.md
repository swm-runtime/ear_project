# Scoring sheet — 100 points

| Axis | Points | Items |

Score the script as it is. Score each item on its own. An ordinary episode gets an ordinary score.

**3.1 Value and depth (12)** — What does the listener understand afterward that they did not before?

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| 10–12 | A real step: the listener gets a mechanism or view about Korea they did not have and would retell. It joins several facts into one answer. Confirming what a newcomer already assumes is not a step. |
| 7–9 | Goes to why and how. Sound and new, with little surprise. |
| 4–6 | One source summarized after another. Correct but empty, or facts about the topic that never answer its question. |
| 0–3 | A list of what things are. No question holds it. |

**3.2 Argument and support (8)** — Is each claim carried by support (what a study did, a case, a number), with the leading source introduced once and limits stated by the speakers?

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| 7–8 | Varied support. The leading source is introduced once and then explained directly. Limits or counterpoints are stated. |
| 5–6 | Support of one kind, or an introduction that sounds mechanical, or attribution on almost every sentence after the introduction. |
| 3–4 | Loose link between claims and support, or a leading source with no introduction. |
| 0–2 | Bare assertion, logical jumps. |

**3.3 Range of views (8)** — Gray areas, trade-offs, disagreement inside Korea.

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| 7–8 | Both sides of a disagreement have material. The conclusion is conditional and leaves the listener room to judge. |
| 5–6 | More than one view, weakly joined, or the other side gets one line and is absorbed. |
| 3–4 | One view, no reflection. |
| 0–2 | Black and white, emotion-led. |

**3.4 Emotional resonance (7)** — Do concrete scenes carry the idea, so a listener far from Korea recognizes a situation of their own?

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| 6–7 | A concrete scene carries the idea. A specific feeling arises. It touches a situation people share. |
| 4–5 | Feeling is named rather than shown. |
| 2–3 | Dry, manual-like. No concrete scene with a person, a place, and a moment anywhere in the episode: at most 3. A one-sentence "imagine a …" line that only restates a rule is not a scene. |
| 0–1 | Preachy or forced. |

**3.5 Opening (6)** — Does the opening seat the listener after the topic is named?

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| 5–6 | Starts from something any listener knows and leaves a question. Before the first explanation, the reason to listen is clear. |
| 3–4 | Correct but flat: the topic, then straight into explanation. An opening that never names the familiar thing the episode will explain: at most 3. |
| 1–2 | Floats without context or sounds templated. |
| 0 | No opening. |

**3.6 Flow and signposts (8)** — One main question through the episode, cases placed on it, a signpost at each change of topic.

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| 7–8 | The main question holds from start to end. Each change of section has a signpost from the content. Each section ends on what it adds to the main question. |
| 5–6 | Complete, but transitions are stiff, or one section drifts, or a section ends on a source summary instead of what it adds. |
| 3–4 | The order of the sources is the structure. The listener loses the main question. |
| 0–2 | Fragments. |

**3.7 Ending (6)** — Does the wrap-up join the sections into one line and answer the main question?

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| 5–6 | Joins each section's answer as one line of reasoning. No new facts or names. The last sentence stays with the listener. |
| 3–4 | A summary that lists ("A, then B, then C") or ends flat. A sign-off that gives advice or a moral caps this at 4. |
| 1–2 | Rushed or weak. |
| 0 | Cut off. |

**3.8 Spoken English (12)** — Read aloud, for a second-language listener.

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| 10–12 | Short sentences, contractions, plain words, Korean words explained on the spot. No stock or formulaic phrasing. At most one written-style sentence. |
| 7–9 | Mostly spoken, with occasional long or written sentences (two main clauses, stacked conditions, dashes, a word a listener would take in another sense). Four or more of these: at most 7. |
| 4–6 | Many written sentences or stock phrases. Readable but hard to follow by ear. Six or more: at most 5. A turn that ends on a sentence restating the turn counts as one each time. |
| 0–3 | Written prose. |

**3.9 Exchange (8)** — Does the rhythm follow the content, and does the host move the conversation?

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| 7–8 | Quick exchanges and long explanations alternate with the content. At least two places where a host line changes the next explanation (a guess the explainer builds on or corrects). An explainer who answers "Right" and continues does not count. |
| 5–6 | Variation is mechanical, or one reaction form repeats four or more times. No point where the host stays partly unconvinced caps this at 6. |
| 3–4 | Strict alternation of explanation and one-line reaction. The host never moves anything. |
| 0–2 | A monologue with interjections. |

**3.10 Immersion (15)** — Do you want to hear the rest, and the next episode? Judge as a whole.

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| 13–15 | Hard to stop. The next episode is wanted. |
| 9–12 | Heard to the end with some slack. An ordinary episode is 9 to 10. Any stretch of three abstract explainer turns, or a list of dates or sources, caps this at 10. |
| 5–8 | Heard, with the mind wandering. An episode with no scene at all: at most 8. |
| 0–4 | Switched off. |

**3.11 Voice and roles (5)** — The explainer sounds like a calm insider. The host sounds like a curious outsider. Roles stay apart.

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| 5 | Two distinct people, no role crossing. |
| 3–4 | Mostly kept; one or two turns blur. The explainer never speaks as an insider (an everyday practice, the Korean name people use, how people around them do it), or the host's non-question turns repeat one reaction form: at most 3. |
| 1–2 | The two sound like one person. |
| 0 | Roles collapse. |

**3.12 The listener's seat (5)** — Does the host stand where a newcomer far from Korea stands, and ask what that listener would ask, where they would get stuck?

| Band | Definition |
|---|---|
| 5 | Host questions and reactions are the listener's. Questions come where the listener gets stuck. Each host turn does one thing. |
| 3–4 | The seat is kept, but questions are placed for convenience, or there are too many questions (every turn, two per turn, a tag question after understanding), negative questions ("Isn't it…?"), or questions about methods or sample sizes. |
| 1–2 | The host acts as if they already know: uses words the explainer has not said yet, or reacts to things never said. |
| 0 | No listener. |
